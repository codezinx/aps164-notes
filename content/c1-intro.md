# C1 - Introduction

## 3 Material Classes

1. **metals:** solids made by the metallic elements (but may also incl. metalloids)
	- i.e. iron, chromium, nickel, steel, brass, zinc, aluminum, copper
	- *metallic bonds* &rarr; Bond Theory
	- *Properties*
		- ductile (can be deformed permanently)
		- electrically and thermally conductive
		- shiny (lustrous)
		- **crystalline:** highly organized at atomic level
		- opaque, strong
2. **ceramics** &mdash; compounds that tend to be hard and brittle (not specific def.)
	- Examples:
		- porcelain, concrete, advanced
		- sapphire (alumina or $\ce{Al2O3}$)
		- quartz (crystalline silica or $\ce{SiO2}$)
		- concrete
		- window glass (soda lime glass)
	- *ionic bonds*
	- often metal oxides
	- *Properties*
		- any opacity (transparent, -lucent, opaque)
		- can be organized or disorganized, **amorphous**
3. **polymers** &mdash; materials that are commonly called "plastics"
	- Examples:
		- Teflon&reg;
		- Gore-Tex&reg;
		- polypropylene (PP)
		- polyethylene (PE)
		- textiles
		- plexiglass (PMMA - polymethylmethacrylate)
		- polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
		- epoxy
		- Styrofoam&reg;
	- *covalent bonds*
	- *Properties*
		- sometimes come from secondary weaker bonds
		- ductile but not strong as metals
		- non-conductive electrically and thermally
		- any opacity

### The Exceptions

- Can be argued to be polymers
	- wood - mainly cellulose, a polymer
	- skin - primarily collagen, a polymer
- **composites:** materials made from mixing materials from 2 different classes
	- i.e. carbon fiber &rarr; fiber in epoxy matrix
	- i.e. fiberglass &rarr; glass fibers embedded in epoxy matrix
- semiconductors
- *allotropes* of pure carbon
	- diamond
	- graphite
	- buckyball
	- carbon nanotubes
