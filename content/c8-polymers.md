# C8 - Polymers

## What is a polymer?

- **polymer:** a type of structure that is made of a lot of repeating units
	- "poly" &mdash; many
	- "mer" &mdash; parts
	- repeating unit: **Mer unit**
	- i.e. "plastics", plastic bags, cell phone cases, cell phone cases, yogurt container, etc.
- **string model of polymer:** polymers can be thought of as long strings of connected atoms
	- or "noodles" / chains (technical term)
- **intramolecular bonds:** bonds that hold atoms together within a molecular
	- a.k.a. *primary (1&deg;) bonds*, *"strong" bonds*
- **intermolecular forces / bonds:** forces that attract one molecule (or chain) to another
	- a.k.a. *secondary (2&deg;) bonds*, *"weak" bonds*

## Molecular Weight &mdash; Length of Molecules

### The "Wooden Box" Game: Get Length of String from Mass

*Situation:* Given a wooden box full of string, a meter of the string, a scale, and mass of box when empty

![Situation](img/c8/c8-wood-box.png)

- *Rules:* You cannot open the box
- *Goal:* Find length of string in box

**How to find length of string:**

1. Measure mass of 1 m string sample
	1. *This gives you:* mass per unit length (i.e. g/meter)
2. Measure mass of box and contents
3. Subtract out mass of box, leaving mass of string
4. Divide: mass of string / mass of 1 m sample = length of string (m)

### Number and Weight Averages

**Number Average Molecular Weight ($\overline{M_n}$):**

$$
\overline{M_n} = \sum^i_{n=1} x_n M_n
$$

- *Note:* there is bar on top of $M_n$
- $i$ &mdash; no. of groups or "bins"
- $\sum^i_{n=1}$ &mdash; tells us sum of terms from 1 until *i*
	- $n$ &mdash; group index
- $M_n$ &mdash; avg. molecular weight of *n*<sup>th</sup> group
- $x_n$ &mdash; number fraction of *n*<sup>th</sup> group

where...

$$
x_n = \frac{n_n}{n_T} = \frac{\text{no. of molecules in group } n}{\text{total no. of molecules in sample}}
$$

**Weight Average Molecular Weight ($\overline{M_w}$):**

$$
\overline{M_w} = \sum^i_{n=1} w_n M_n
$$

- *Note:* there is bar on top of $M_w$
- common variables retain common meaning
- $w_n$ &mdash; weight fraction of *n*<sup>th</sup> group

where...

$$
w_n = \frac{m_n}{m_T} = \frac{\text{avg. mass of molecules in group } n}{\text{total mass of sample}}

\newcommand{\Ds}{Đ}
$$

**Dispersity ($\Ds$):**

$$
\Ds = \frac{\overline{M_w}}{\overline{M_n}}
$$

indicates how broad distribution is

### Example Calculation of Averages

*Hypothetical polymer:*

![Polymer strings](img/c8/c8-hypot-poly.png)

Composed of:

- 5 short (black) &mdash; 5,000 g/mol
- 6 medium (blue) &mdash; 8,000 g/mol
- 9 long (red) &mdash; 11,000 g/mol
- 4 very long (yellow) &mdash; 15,000 g/mal

**Find number molecular weight:**

Total no. of molecules: $n_T = 5 + 6 + 9 + 4 = 24$

*Way 1: the average*

$$
\begin{align*}
\overline{M_n} &= \frac{5(5,000) + 6(8,000) + 9(11,000) + 4(15,000)}{24} \\
&\doteq 9,666\text{ g/mol}
\end{align*}
$$

*Way 2: Using the equation; same as way 1*

$$
\begin{align*}
\overline{M_n} &= \sum^4_{n=1} x_n M_n \\
&= \frac 5{24}(5,000) + \frac 6{24}(8,000) + \frac 9{24}(11,000) + \frac 4{24}(15,000) \\
&\doteq 9,666\text{ g/mol}
\end{align*}
$$

**Find weight average molecular weight:**

Total weight: $m_T = 5(5,000) + 6(8,000) + 9(11,000) + 4(15,000) = 232,000\text{ g/mol}$

$$
\begin{align*}
\overline{M_w} &= \sum^4_{n=1} w_n M_n \\
&= \frac{5(5,000)}{232,000}(5,000) + \frac{6(8,000)}{232,000}(8,000) + {...} + \frac{4(15,000)}{232,000}(15,000) \\
&\doteq 10,767 \text{ g/mol}
\end{align*}
$$

*NOTE:* Weight average mol. weight higher than number average

## Relaxation Modulus and Transition Temperatures ($T_g, T_m$)

- when load applied ($\sigma_0$) &rarr; observed strain changing
- *experimentally:* apply fixed strain $\epsilon_0$
	- then observe stress relaxing w/ time &rarr; $\sigma(t)$
- i.e. nylon strings on guitar
	- "relax" over time

**Relaxation Modulus ($E_R$):**

$$
E_R = \frac{\sigma(t)}{\epsilon_0} = \frac{\text{stress over time}}{\text{init. fixed strain}}
$$

## Intro to Viscoelasticity (Time Dependency)

**Silly-Putty&reg; and Key Point**

- *viscoelasticity* best shown in Silly-Putty&reg;
	- putty-like mat. made from polydimethylsiloxane
	- when stretched slowly, stretches like thick gooey liquid (lots of plastic deform.)
	- when rolled into ball, bounces like rubber ball (no perm. deform.)
	- when rolled into cylinder and pulled apart very quickly
		- snaps like brittle material
	- due to rel. short molecules in the putty
- *KEY POINT:* sensitive to strain rate

**Heavy Desk and Carpet Example**

Diagram of time dependent deformation using heavy desk and carpet:

![Diagram](img/c8/c8-desk-carpet.png)

- carpet made of many polymer fibers oriented roughly vertically
- if desk left on it for several months, low spots in carpet forms
	- *viscous* &mdash; time-dependent
- if only left on it for couple seconds, polymers spring back
	- *elastic* &mdash; instantaneous
- **viscous deformation:** long time-scale deformation
	- ref. to flow of liquid
- **viscoelastic deformation:** property of being able to elastically and viscously deform
	- *Ranges:* high strength epoxy (close to elastic) &rarr; Silly-Putty&reg; (close to viscous)

## Optical Transparency: Limits of String Model

Crystalline and amorphous regions of polymers have different indices of refraction, diagram:

![Diagram](img/c8/c8-semicrystal-ind-ref.png)

- **scattering events** of light occurs in crystalline structure
	- *semicrystalline* mats. appear translucent / opaque
- for transparency, polymer can't be semicrystalline
	- polymer *never* 100% crystalline
	- *remaining option:* 100% amorphous polymer

**Solution of Plexiglas&reg;** &mdash; polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA)

Mer unit of PMMA, and large side group:

![Diagram](img/c8/c8-pmma-em-sg.png)

- large side group prevents crystallization
- scattering events don't occur as often &rarr; transparency

**The catch, other examples:**

- sapphire ($\ce{Al2O3}$) &mdash; *crystalline* &mdash; clear and transparent
- window glass &mdash; amorphous &mdash; clear and transparent
- polycrystalline metal &mdash; opaque
- glassy (amorphous) metals &mdash; opaque

**Limitations:**

- need to know what visible light is
- string model cannot describe interaction of light w/ molecule
- visible light interacts w/ electrons in matter
